KINKAY/English/Headshots & portraits

Makeup for headshots and portraits

A headshot is looked at from thirty centimetres on a phone screen. That is a completely different problem from an evening look, and it is where most event makeup falls apart.

Check my date See prices
The problem

A lens at one metre sees everything

Event makeup is built for distance and low light. A headshot is the opposite: a sharp lens close to the face, even light, and an image that will be retouched afterwards and then looked at on a phone.

  • The base holds texture instead of erasing it. Heavy powder photographs as a mask at close range. Skin that still reads as skin is what makes a portrait look expensive.
  • No visible edges. Jawline, hairline, the corner of the eye — anywhere a product stops is where a close lens finds it.
  • Easier for the retoucher. Kay finishes a face so the photographer's edit is light. Over-contoured makeup takes longer to fix than no makeup at all.
  • Consistency across a team. For company shoots, everyone should look like they were photographed on the same day by the same person. That is a makeup decision as much as a lighting one.
